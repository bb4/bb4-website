Graduate School

In June 1990 I left the east coast and went to Calidfornia. I started life as a student-employee at UC Davis at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL). My adviser was Nelson Max.

The summers were fun because there were lots of interns to hang out with and do waterskiing, river tubing, camping, volleyball, and other activities with. Winters were less exciting. Mostly I practiced at Aikido of Livermore where Jim Alvarez was the head instructor.

Throughout my youth, I have been very active physically. As a result, I have had many close calls and near death experiences. I think my neck, back, and joints will always have some pain as a result of the activities I partook of in my youth.

My sister had arrived in California a few years before me and was living with her boyfriend in San Francisco. I occasionally went to visit them in the city. For a while, they lived in a loft in a huge warehouse in the SOMA area. They had some big parties, but it was kind of a seedy area. Pam's boyfriend, Dave, would often have to go out to the street and chase people away who were trying to break into cars.

I went to martial arts seminars, and on Sundays often went to Sunday sparring in Oakland. It was held first at a Kajukenbo school and later at Baker's Tae Kwon Do. For just 2 dollars, you could fight complete strangers with unknown backgrounds and varying experience. I met a lot of very interesting people at these gatherings - all of them great. Sometimes people got hit very hard. It was a foolish, risky thing to do in hindsight. Fortunately, I survived these experiences.

While living in Livermore, and working an LLNL, I lived at a number of different locations. At first I rented a room in a house with an older divorced woman. There was also another tenant there. He was an intern at the lab. I got along fine with the other tenant, but the owner was always asking us to help her do odd jobs or watch TV with her. When I left, she kept the security deposit, even though I had cleaned everything out.

I bought a used, red Nissan 200sx sports car (with stich shift) for $5000. It was a newer version of the Datsun 200sx that I had bought from Rich for $1 the last year of RPI. I really liked the car. I took it on many tris to yosoemit, camping, etc. It would last many years. I later gave it to Shanna when she needed a car. Ultimately, it died because we forgot to change the timing belt before it needed it.

Next, I lived at a house even closer to the lab, with 2 other physics grad students. There was a heavy bag mounted in the garage. That was nice, and I used it a lot. It was around this time that I met my first girlfriend, Barbara. She was employed as a nanny for one of the UC Davis professors. I met her at one of the grad student mixers. At first I thought she was one of the grad students. We spoke for a bit there, and later she called me. We drove to different places in my Nissan and had a great time that summer, but then she went home to Wyoming.

When I was 27, in 1994, I tore my ACL after an aikdo class. Michael Speece had asked me how to do spinning kicks. I tried to explain by jumping in the air, rotating 360 degrees, and landing on 2 feet, without the kick. If I had done the kick, it would have been fine, but without the kick, there was nothing to stop the rotational inertia of my body except my foot when it hit the ground. That force went into my knee. The muscles in my knee was likely already fatiqued from an evening of aikido practice. There was a loud crack as my ACL gave way. Shanna helped me limp home. At the time, we lived in an apartment close to the dojo.

I went to the orthopedist a day or two later and he gave me the options. In hindsight I probably should have just skipped the reconstructive surgery. It didn't work out that well. The Dr removed one third of my pateller tendon and used that to replace the broken ACL. It took six months to recover. Shanna was very supportive during that time. It would have been even more difficult without her. I probably was too aggressive in the things I did to try and recover. At one point I remember riding my bike up the face of mission peak. Riding down I had more than one fall over the front of my handle bars. Fortunately, I was able to do aikido rolls to save myself from a broken neck, but I was wearing a knee brace and its possible it did more damag. I was able to return to aikido after 6 months, but when I went to see the orthopedist, he told me that the it was still like I did not have an ACL. I could definitely feel some instability in my knee when doing some activities. A few years later, I decided to do the surgery again with a different Dr. This time, they decided to use a cadaver tendon - the tendon from a dead guy - likely killed on a motorcycle. The surgery seemed to go better, but a few years later, another orthopedist told me that it was like the ACL was not there. After that, I decided to just live with it.

At one point the lack of ACL caused me to get a bucket handle tear in the meniscus of that knee. This is a very painful condition where, when you bend your knee, the meniscus buckles inward into the knee causing pain. As I slowly unbent my knee, the tendon sould suddenly unbuckle, causing more pain. The surgeon trimmed out the buckle, but then I was missing much of the meniscus that is used to pad and protect the knee. Later I had another surgery to remove painful scar tissue. Then decades later, I had a bike accident, tearing the meniscus in my other knee. Through all this, I continued to practice aidkido and do other activities, but I need to careful. Shanna reminds me that I am not careful.

Somewhere around my second year at LLNL, I decided to go to the main UC Davis campus. Ostensibly, it was to take some classes that were not offered at the lab, but really it was so that I could meet more women. There were way more eligible women at the campus than at the UC extension in Livermore. I sublet a room in an apartment with 2 other students. It was wonderful being on campus. There were so many bicycles. I had a nice bike, and rode to classes along with every one else. There were rivers of bicycles. My nice bicycle was stolen on like the second day. Then I got a crappy bike, and it lasted much longer. I practiced aikido and took gymnastics. I learned to do a forward handspring - which is quite an accomplishment for someone who is 6 foot. I very nearly broke my neck trying to do a backward handspring, however. There were many attractive women at gymnastics. I went on dates with two of them. I don't think I did the date thing very well because both were just single dates. My follow up was not very good. I also went on a date with another woman I met at a party there, but that also wasn't going to work out because it turned out that she was married.

I met Jeff Greenberg at aikido. He had a shodan from Chiba sensei in San Diego. He told me lot sof fun sotries about how brutal Chiba was to some of his students. Not sure if the stories were true, but they were entertaining.

When I returned from UC Davis, I rented a room in a house on the west side of Livermore from Seth Abrams. Seth was an employee at the Lab. I started traveling to Fremont evenings to practice aikido there. Sunny and his wife, Miki, were just starting a large Aikido dojo there.

It was around October of 1992 that I met Shanna. I already knew her sister, Kristie, through aikido. Shanna was one of 8 sisters and 4 brothers - many of which praticed at the aikido school there in Fremont. I became close with the family, and convinced Shanna to return to aikido. For our first date, I borrowed Kristie's van and took Shanna and several meices and nephews (Sterling, Crystal, Chrissie, and Jennifer) down to Milpitas to see Disney's "Aladan" and go to Sweet Tomatoes afterward. It was a great first date. We both felt "electricity" when our hands touched reaching for the popcorn. The kids were well behaved, and helped smooth over any awkwardness in communication. Shanna was just learning English at the time as she had just come to California from Vietnam less than a year ago.

In December 1992 I graduated from UC Davis with an MS degree in Computer Science. Also, at this time, I got another BS degree in mathematics from Rensselaer. I presented the results of my masters' thesis Smooth Transitions Between Bump Rendering Algorithms at SIGGRAPH 93.
