Retirement

Early retirement

This retirement phase of life is just beginning for me in January 2026. I have high hopes that it will be fun. I am still in my 50's, so still have capabilities and can enjoy a lot of activities. I am concerned about later retirement when all the health problems start to rear their ugly heads.

In the first year of my retirement I played more go, practiced more aikido, and developed more than a dozen multiplayer online games. I have enjoyed using AI to create the games and refactor many of my old software projects. It makes me many times more productive. I use my YouTube channel to announce each game to the world as I release it.

Shanna and I get to spend a lot of time together now. We take BART to different cities and go for long hikes or bikes followed by lunch at a restaurant. We can't go anywhere too fancy because I smuggle in avocdadoes to have my my lunch. for some reason I need to have avocado at every meal. At first she didn't want me to retire because she feared I would bother her too much, but we are getting along fine so far.

Life is much the same as before retiring, except now I work for myself rather than a company. I have endless computer projects to work on. There is more time for doing cooking, biking, playing with Chip (my cat) and working in the back yard. I got a season pass to Great America for the first time.

Unlike some, I am very excited by the prospects of AI. I think it is most likely going to lead to an abundant future - right up until the point when it kills us all. With any luck humanity will be able to merge with the AI before that happens. In late 2025 I created this short YouTube video describing my view of mankind's past and future based on this spreadsheet.

One recent project that I worked on that gave me a lot of satisfaction was Into the Singularity. It's a grand strategy simulation game that allows players to explore what is coming in the future. Unfortunately, I haven't found anyone who is interested in playing it yet. Some say it is too confusing or overwhelming. I tried it with my cousin Lynette, and she seemed very stressed as she played - almost like it was tortuous for her. Perhaps someone reading this will be brave enough to click the link and give it a try.

I like to debate people on what the future will hold. My predictions tend to be on the optimistic side. My son cautions me that there are probably a lot of frictions in the physical, political, and regulatory world that will likely slow things down a lot. That is probably true. I see digital advancements far outpacing the physical and biological worlds. Eventually the digital world will merge with or displace the real one.
