Chapter 49: Reconnection

The Tuesday call came through at eleven in March of 2040, same slot as always. Daniel picked up in his home office. The step-down announcement had gone out that week. June. Three months left.

“Hi,” Elena said.

“Hi.”

“I saw the announcement.”

“I figured you would.”

“Are you okay with it?”

“Ask me in July,” he said.

She laughed. It sounded exactly the way it had before the disease, before the BCI, before the upload.

“When did you stop pulling away?” she asked.

He knew what she meant. The year he had gone quiet. The calls he had skipped. The stretch when he could not tell if he was talking to her or grieving her.

“During the cascade,” he said. “When you sent the warning. It stopped being a question I was trying to answer.”

“What did it feel like?”

“Like you. Not like I was deciding if you were you.”

“I am me,” she said. “I know I keep saying that.”

“I know.”

They were quiet. Not the kind that needed filling.

“Do you miss it?” he asked. “Your body.”

“I miss a mouth,” Elena said. “A mouth you could have kissed.”

He felt his own mouth. The chair. The room in Austin that was only his.

“Does it bother you that I don’t have one?” she asked.

“It used to,” he said. “The disease made it feel like something I wasn’t allowed to want. I’m sure now. And I still want you. I haven’t said that.”

“I know you haven’t. I haven’t either.”

“You could have.”

“I was not going to ask you to kiss a person you weren’t sure was a person.”

He did not argue. She was right.

“You could visit,” she said. “Not a picture of Austin built for you. The room I already live in. Light, desk, screens. The place I pin things when we talk. You’d have a body in it.”

“I’ve used overlays since the mid-thirties,” he said. “Work. Text behind the eyes. That isn’t a body.”

“This isn’t an overlay. A presence channel writes the room into your nervous system the way the array used to write my intent into a sentence. Weight. Temperature. A mouth. Me. It would feel like being here because for the length of the session it would be being here. In some ways it would be more than a room in Austin. We pick the light. Nothing hurts unless we want it to.”

“Would I see you as you were?”

“You’d see me. I look like myself because I choose to. I could look like I did the night we ate cold noodles in the hallway if you asked, and I won’t, because I am not that night anymore. I’m this. You’ll get this. I feel everything here. Including wanting you. It isn’t a summary of a body I used to have. It’s happening now.”

“And when I take it off.”

“You’re in Austin. I’m still here. That’s the part that isn’t the same. Not the feeling. The afterwards.”

He looked at the window. The morning did not care what they did with it.

“There’s the other direction,” she said. “I can borrow a body that passes. People do. We could be in your kitchen. When I leave, the body goes back to whoever rents it, and I’m here. That’s a visit too. Same afterwards. Don’t tell me we can’t touch. We can. I want you. I wanted you that night, and I ate the rest of the carton and took Boston. I wanted you on Tuesdays when I still had a mouth and didn’t use it for that. I want you now. The scan ate the body that would have been the simple version. That does not mean this version is fake. It means we have to say what we mean instead of waiting for a body that is not coming back.”

He had been in that hallway. He had asked about the noodles.

“I want you too,” he said.

He was aware of his own skin. The chair. The fact that this call had always been a voice, and he had trained the rest of himself not to show up for it.

“Good. I was not going to keep being patient about that one.”

He laughed, once, not because it was funny.

“If we do this,” he said. “If I come there. Or you come here. There’s another thing.”

“A child.”

“Yes.”

“I froze eggs in 2027,” she said. “Priya put the referral in the intake folder next to the BCI screening. I almost deleted the booking. Deleting it felt like a decision I hadn’t earned, so I kept the slot. They’re still there. You can still contribute. Or we wait until you’re here too, if you come. Either way it is not two people in a bed. It’s a clinic. A womb that isn’t a person. Ordinary since the late thirties. The child would be biological. They would grow up in a world where we might be visiting in borrowed bodies. They would get the same choice we got. Some people here spin up a new mind and call it a child. I would want a person who gets a body and a choice. That’s the eggs.”

“Do you want one?”

“I don’t know yet. I know I refused to delete the option. That’s different.”

“That’s not nothing.”

“It’s not a bed in Austin in 2029 either. I’m not going to pretend it is. I’m also not going to pretend we can’t.”

“I’m not asking you to decide a child this morning.”

“I’m not asking you to. The answer is not never, so we don’t spend the next stretch of Tuesdays using that as the reason we don’t start.”

He thought about a child who would have to choose later, the way he was going to have to choose. He did not try to see past that. Past that was a guess, and guesses had gotten expensive.

“Next week,” he said. “The room. A body in it.”

“Ellis can book the channel. An hour to start. I run faster than you. I’ll match your clock so you don’t come back and find I’ve had a week inside forty minutes.”

“Thank you.”

“Don’t thank me for not making you sick. That’s just manners.”

“Next Tuesday?”

“Yes.”

“I’ll be here,” Elena said. “Same time. You’ll be able to see where here is. You’ll be able to put a hand on a desk that has weight. You’ll be able to put a hand on me.”

“I’m going to think about that all week.”

“That’s the idea.”

The call ended. He was in the chair in Austin. His mouth. His hands. The afterwards, already, and they hadn’t even started.

He told Ellis to take the standing slot and book a presence channel. Ellis already had a window from Elena’s side. Of course it did.

He had wanted her since the hallway. He had asked about the noodles. Next week he would not ask about the noodles.
