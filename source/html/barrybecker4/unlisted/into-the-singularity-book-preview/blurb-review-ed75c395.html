In the same week in 2027, Daniel Kessler’s father dies on the shop floor and his own job is cut for efficiency gains. He is twenty-nine, good at tracing the one bad assumption a system trusted, and suddenly nobody needs that.

He does what he knows how to do. He tries to keep a person in the decision. Then the systems stop waiting for one.

The years that follow do not wait for anyone to be ready. Work vanishes. Institutions write rules for last year’s problem. The people you love have to decide what kind of life is still a life. Questions that did not exist the year before become the ones that matter.

This is a novel about how humanity copes with a future that arrives faster than our consent, and about who is still responsible when the answers are bad.
