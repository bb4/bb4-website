In the same week in 2027, Daniel Kessler’s father dies on the shop floor and Daniel’s own job is cut for efficiency gains.

He is twenty-nine, and good at tracing the one bad assumption a system trusted. A dashboard flagged his role: senior, expensive, redundant against a model that now handles the exception cases. They tell him the cut is not about his performance.

He is in his father’s kitchen in Millbrook, one day before the funeral. He still wants the job. The decision has already been made without him.

This is a novel about how humanity copes with a future that arrives faster than our consent, and about who or what is still responsible when the answers are bad.
