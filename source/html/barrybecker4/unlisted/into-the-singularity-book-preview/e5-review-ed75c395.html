Epilogue: Reunion

Six months later. July 2042.

Daniel and Elena sat at a cafe table in a city that did not exist on Earth.

They had written the prompt in April, three months after he crossed. One sentence they argued over for a week of their time. On Earth that week was a moment of April. Elena’s version won, mostly: a city no planning office on Earth could have signed. Other people lived in it. They had to walk it to get a cup of coffee.

The system that shaped substrate space built the city from that sentence. Towers branched and rejoined. Streets ran at angles that would be falling on Earth. Light came from nowhere a sun would be. A river climbed the face of a wall, because they had asked it to. Water went downhill unless you told it not to.

They had lived in Elena’s workspace those first weeks. Desk, light, screens. That room still existed. They came here instead.

The cafe sat on a street that did not attach to a building in any way a beam would understand. Other tables were full. Uploaded people in bodies they had chosen. Personal AIs, the same class as Ellis, walking in bodies of their own. You could not always tell which was which unless you asked. A woman in a jacket whose cut kept changing nodded at Elena. Elena nodded back. At the next table a man laughed at something his companion had not said.

Elena set two cups down. The coffee tasted like coffee because they had asked it to. Neither of them needed it. They drank it anyway.

“How was the visit?” she asked.

“Mei beat me,” he said. “She gave me a handicap of nine stones and still won.”

He had been in Wei’s kitchen the week before, in a body with his face and his voice and the weight he put in a chair. Lin had rolled the wrappers. Mei was home from Toronto for the month. Wei took a bowl off the upper shelf with his right arm and set it where Daniel could reach it. They made dumplings. Daniel ate one. It tasted like the dumpling. After the dishes Mei got the board out from under the window. Wei played with his right hand. She set nine stones in front of Daniel.

Mei said the building category had come off that spring, a year after the passport, and the shoulder the year before. Then she went back to clearing the stones.

His fingers closed correctly around the first stone. Pressure. Warmth. The click when he set it down. Those fingers had been made for the visit. The hand he had been born with was the tissue the scan consumed in January. Wei watched the stone go down. He did not ask. Daniel set the next stone down after it. He did not tell Elena.

“She’s publishing,” he said. “The construction is Wei’s, in the references. Her name is on the paper.”

“Good,” Elena said.

She turned her cup. A pair at the far table argued over a map that rearranged while they held it.

“The clinic wrote,” Elena said. “An opening in September. The eggs from Bridge. Your sample from the bank. A child born in a body.”

“They sequenced me in 2027. I carry one of the inherited genes for ALS, the disease that was taking away my ability to move. My sister doesn’t have it. They take that gene out of the embryo before there is a person. The child will not have that gene. They don’t touch anything else. Disease, and nothing past it.”

“September,” she said.

“I want the child,” he said.

She waited.

“I love you,” he said.

It was the first time he had said it. Elena looked at him across the cups.

“I love you too.”

They finished the coffee. His hand stayed on the cup. The river went on up the wall.
